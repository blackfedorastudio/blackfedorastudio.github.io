How to Print Wall Art at Home

Bought a digital print and not sure what to do next? This guide explains frame ratios, paper, printer settings and print shops, so your poster looks as good on the wall as it does on screen.

1. Match the ratio to your frame

Digital posters usually come in a few ratios. Each ratio covers several frame sizes, because the picture simply scales up or down:

  • 2:3 for 4x6, 8x12, 12x18, 16x24, 20x30 and 24x36 in
  • 3:4 for 6x8, 9x12, 12x16, 15x20 and 18x24 in
  • 4:5 for 8x10 and 16x20 in
  • 11x14 for 11x14 in frames
  • ISO for A5, A4, A3, A2 and A1

Measure the opening of your frame (or the mat), then pick the file with the matching ratio. If the ratio does not match, the picture will be cropped or get white edges.

2. Choose the right paper

  • Matte photo paper or heavy matte paper (200 to 250 gsm): the best all-round choice for posters. No glare, rich colours.
  • Card stock: good for small prints, cards and party signs.
  • Glossy photo paper: very bright colours, but it reflects light behind glass.
  • Plain printer paper: fine for a test print, not for the final poster.

3. Printer settings that matter

  1. Open the file in a PDF or image viewer, not in a browser.
  2. Set the scale to 100% / Actual size, not "Fit to page", if the file is already the paper size.
  3. Choose the paper type you are actually using (for example "Matte photo paper").
  4. Set quality to High or Best.
  5. Print one small test first to check the colours.

4. When to use a print shop

Most home printers stop at A4 or US Letter. For anything bigger, upload the file to a local print shop or an online print service. Ask for matte poster paper and send the highest-resolution file. Prints up to 24 x 36 in are easy to order this way, and often cheaper than you would expect.

5. Why colours can look different

Screens glow, paper reflects light. Prints usually look a little darker and softer than on a bright phone screen. Turning your screen brightness down before judging a test print gives you a more realistic preview.

6. Framing tips

  • A mat (passe-partout) makes even a small print look premium.
  • Black or natural wood frames suit retro and vintage prints.
  • For a gallery wall, keep 5 to 8 cm between frames and align the centre line at eye level.
